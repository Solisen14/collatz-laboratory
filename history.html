Defining the Collatz Function

This introductory mathematical explanation has been moved here temporarily while the History section is developed.

Begin with any positive integer. If the number is even, divide it by 2. If it is odd, multiply it by 3 and add 1. Because the rule depends on the input, we describe the process with a piecewise-defined function.

The notation C(n) means the result of applying the Collatz rule once to n. Every positive integer is either odd or even, so exactly one case applies.

For example, starting at 8 gives C(8) = 8 ÷ 2 = 4. Starting at 3 gives C(3) = 3(3) + 1 = 10.
