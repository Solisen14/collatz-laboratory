WORKSPACE · 02

Circular Representation

Explore a circular tree built from real forward Collatz orbits. The value 1 is the central bullseye, with distinct number paths branching outward toward the rim. Select any node to follow its orbit back to 1.

How many starting integers?

Canvas appearance

Cyan when selected: even step, divide by 2 Gold when selected: odd step, 3n + 1 Rim labels: endpoints

How to read the circle. The gold-ringed 1 is at the exact centre. Coloured dots are distinct integers, and fine connections link consecutive Collatz values. The outer branches end at labelled endpoints on the rim. Position and spacing make the branching structure visible; they do not encode the integer's magnitude. Follow the arrows of a selected orbit inward toward 1. A dashed return arc from 1 to 4 closes the familiar cycle.

WORKSPACE · 03

Angle-Path Trails

Instead of connecting numbers as nodes, transform complete Collatz orbits into drawn curves. Even and odd values make the pencil turn in opposite directions. When orbits share the same later steps, their curves retrace common paths.

Change the colours without changing the paths.

Determines how step lengths depend on the values visited.

Controls how sharply parity changes the direction.

How the curves are constructed

Start each curve at 1 and follow a Collatz orbit backward toward its original starting number. Each value r determines both a step length and a left or right turn:

Length = r / (1 + re)

Turn = aπ (log(2)/log(6) − (r mod 2))

Both formulas come from the Wolfram Community example. The curves are an artistic encoding of the orbits, not literal paths of integers through space.
