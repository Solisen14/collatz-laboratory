HOW TO READ THIS EXPERIMENT

Finite paths. Open questions.

Only positive starts. We use starting numbers 1 to 25, positive a and b, and ordinary integer arithmetic. All subsequent values are positive.

A shared directed graph. Every integer is one node, even when multiple starting values visit it. Each arrow points to the next value; cyclic arrows are shown explicitly.

Stopping rules. We continue through 1 and stop at the first repeated value, after 200 steps, or before exceeding 1,000,000,000,000. A repeated value closes a cycle; the outcome tells us whether that cycle contains 1. The map draws at most the first 32 edges per starting value, while the inspector reports the calculated path and its outcome. Reaching a boundary does not establish that a path tends to infinity.

No proof by picture. Changing a and b creates different dynamical systems. Seeing 25 paths does not establish what happens for all starting values.
